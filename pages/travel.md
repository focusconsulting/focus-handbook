# Travel Policy

_Effective October 2025  ·  Approved by the Executive Team_

## Client-Site Travel

Focus is remote-first, and travel is part of the job. As consultants, our work happens in partnership with government stakeholders, and that occasionally means being in the room with them. Client-site travel is a normal and expected part of working at Focus.

This is tied directly to delivery success and client relationships, which is what keeps contracts healthy and the business running.

### What "Occasional" Actually Means

Travel frequency varies by contract and client:

- **Most contracts:** infrequent travel. A kickoff, a milestone review, an on-site visit once or twice a year.
- **Some clients:** more regular in-person presence. The Department of State, for example, often expects weekly or near-weekly attendance for staff local to the site. This is the nature of that client relationship.
- **Contract leads** should expect the highest likelihood of travel, as they are typically the primary point of contact and relationship owner with the client.

### Why It Matters

Some things don't translate well over Slack or a video call, including trust-building, reading a room, and catching what a client isn't saying directly. Physical presence is sometimes the fastest way to solve a problem or strengthen a relationship that benefits the whole team. When client relationships weaken, it directly affects contract continuity.

### What We Ask

- **At staffing time:** If a contract involves more frequent travel, that should be made clear up front. It is fair to ask about expected travel cadence during staffing conversations.
- **If real constraints exist:** Health, caregiving, or other family circumstances are real constraints. Talk to your contract lead or People Operations and we will work with you.
- **On discomfort vs. constraint:** General discomfort with travel or in-person work is understandable, but it is not a hard constraint. As a business with client obligations, we need staff who can flex when the work calls for it.

## Overview & Approval

This policy applies to all employees and contractors traveling on behalf of Focus Consulting. Most work is conducted virtually. When in-person travel is necessary for client meetings, conferences, or team events, this policy governs how travel is planned, booked, and reimbursed.

> **Pre-Approval Required:** All travel plans must be approved before booking. Any expense that falls outside the guidelines below requires prior written approval from Meg Greenberg, Business Operations. Unapproved expenses may not be reimbursed.

## Flights

- **Rate alignment:** Airfare should align with GSA rates for the destination and travel type
- **Pre-approval:** All flights require approval from Meg Greenberg before booking
- **Domestic travel:** Economy class is required
- **International travel (6+ hours):** Premium economy or business class is permitted with manager approval
- **Flight changes:** Advance approval is required for changes unless an emergency occurs
- **Frequent flyer programs:** Personal mileage programs are allowed, provided the booking remains cost-effective

## Hotels & Lodging

- **Rate alignment:** Hotel expenses must align with GSA lodging rates for the city
- **Rates exceeding GSA limits** require prior approval
- **Preferred standard:** Single rooms in 3–4 star hotels
- **Reimbursable incidentals:** Parking and Wi-Fi are reimbursable
- **Non-reimbursable:** Minibar, in-room entertainment, and personal amenities are not reimbursable

## Meals & Per Diem

- **Per diem:** Meals and incidental expenses are reimbursed based on GSA meal and incidental expense (M&IE) rates for the travel destination
- **Receipts:** Required when actual expenses exceed the per diem rate
- **Alcohol:** Alcoholic beverages are only reimbursed for pre-approved client entertainment
- **Group meals:** The most senior Focus employee present should pay and submit the receipt with attendee names noted

### GSA Per Diem Reference

Current GSA per diem rates by city are available at [gsa.gov/travel/plan-book/per-diem-rates](https://www.gsa.gov/travel/plan-book/per-diem-rates). When in doubt, use the standard continental U.S. (CONUS) rate.

## Ground Transportation

| Type | Policy |
| --- | --- |
| Rideshare (Uber, Lyft) | Preferred for airport transfers and local travel |
| Public transportation | Preferred when practical and available |
| Rental car | Mid-size or smaller unless client needs or group size justifies larger; requires pre-approval |
| Personal vehicle | Reimbursed at the current IRS standard mileage rate; receipts not required but mileage must be logged |
| Taxi | Acceptable when rideshare is unavailable; receipts required |
| Parking | Reimbursable with receipt; use airport economy lots when possible |

## Reimbursement Process

1. **Collect receipts** for all reimbursable expenses during travel
2. **Submit all receipts to Meg Greenberg within 14 days** of returning from travel
3. **Documentation required** for: airfare, hotel stays, meals exceeding per diem, ground transportation, and any other significant expenses
4. **Reimbursement** is processed with the next regular payroll cycle following submission

> **14-Day Submission Window:** All receipts must be submitted within 14 days of travel. Late submissions may be denied or subject to additional review. If you anticipate difficulty meeting this deadline, contact Meg Greenberg in advance.

### Required Documentation Summary

| Expense Type | Documentation Required |
| --- | --- |
| Airfare | Itinerary and receipt showing amount paid |
| Hotel | Itemized hotel folio |
| Meals (over per diem) | Receipt with business purpose and attendees noted |
| Ground transportation | Receipt or mileage log |
| Rental car | Rental agreement and receipt |
| Other expenses | Receipt and brief description |

## Non-Reimbursable Expenses

The following expenses will not be reimbursed:

- Personal entertainment (movies, sporting events, sightseeing)
- Excessive meals or alcohol beyond what is permitted above
- Traffic or parking fines
- Unapproved seat upgrades or cabin class upgrades
- Expenses for personal companions or family members
- Lost or stolen personal property
- Expenses submitted more than 14 days after travel without prior approval

## Emergency Travel Changes

For travel interruptions, delays, or emergencies while traveling, contact Meg Greenberg as soon as possible. Emergency expenses (rebooking, emergency lodging, etc.) will be handled on a case-by-case basis. Document all emergency expenses and submit with a brief written explanation upon return.

## Compliance

Employees and contractors are expected to use good judgment and act as responsible stewards of company resources when traveling. Non-compliance with this policy, including submitting unsupported or inaccurate expense reports, may result in denied reimbursements and disciplinary action up to and including termination.

Questions about travel approval, expense eligibility, or reimbursement? Contact **Meg Greenberg** in Business Operations.
