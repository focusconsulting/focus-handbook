# Work Schedule & Overtime Policy

_Effective October 2025  ·  Approved by the Executive Team_

## Our Standard: A 40-Hour Week

Focus works a 40-hour week.

We recognize that government contracting occasionally requires flexibility beyond standard hours. These periods of extended hours are the exception, not the rule.

> **Consistent overtime usually means understaffing or scope creep:** If any team member finds themselves consistently working over 40 hours per week, address the underlying cause: resource constraints, scope creep, unrealistic timelines, or process inefficiencies. Raise these issues through retrospectives, program health checks, and escalation to Focus leadership.

## Standard Work Week

### Full-Time Employees

| Parameter | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Hours per week | 40–45 hours |
| Work days | Monday through Friday |
| Core hours | 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM Eastern Time (may vary by contract) |
| Work environment | Fully remote with occasional travel for client meetings, conferences, or team events |

### Flexible Work Arrangements

Focus recognizes the need for flexibility to accommodate client schedules across time zones, government contract requirements, personal work-life balance, and project-specific demands. Discuss any flexibility needs with your project lead and practice lead.

## Salaried Exempt Employees

### Professional Responsibilities

Exempt employees are expected to fulfill their professional duties regardless of the specific number of hours required, including meeting project deadlines, attending client meetings, participating in proposals and business development, and ensuring quality delivery of contracted services.

### Occasional Extended Hours

Government contracting requires flexibility. Exempt employees should expect to occasionally work beyond 40-hour weeks for the following scenarios:

| Scenario | Typical Duration |
| --- | --- |
| Proposal submissions | 2–4 weeks of extended hours during preparation and submission |
| Project go-live events | System implementations, data migrations, or launch activities |
| Client presentations | Preparation for and delivery of executive briefings or milestone presentations |
| Government inspections | Contract audits, site visits, or compliance reviews |
| End-of-contract activities | Final deliverable preparation and transition activities |

### Frequency Guidelines

- **Expected frequency:** 4–6 periods per year requiring extended hours
- **Typical duration:** 1–3 weeks per period
- **Maximum consecutive period:** 4 weeks without executive review and approval
- **Recovery time:** Following extended periods, supervisors should accommodate flexible scheduling when operationally feasible

### Work-Life Balance Commitments

- Providing as much advance notice as possible for known extended hours
- Distributing extended hours across team members when possible
- Allowing reduced hours or flexible scheduling during non-critical periods
- Protecting allocated professional development and training time

## Compensatory Time (Comp Time)

Comp time exists to offset periods of unusually large amounts of work and sustained long hours. Comp time should be relied on rarely, and regular use of comp time indicates that more structural changes are needed on a project.

### Eligibility

- **You must work as an exempt employee on a Time & Materials (T&M) contract** where your time is billable to a client. You are not eligible for comp time if you work on a Firm Fixed Price (FFP) contract or on internal work.
- **Clients (and primes) must specifically request that Focus employees work more than 40 hours** in a given week (or more than an average of 8 hrs/day for shorter weeks).
- **Focus employees must bill 44 or more hours in a given week** to be eligible for comp time that week (or more than an average of 9 hrs/day for shorter weeks).
  - This means 44 hours actually worked. Holidays, PTO, and sick time are not included in the total hours worked for the week.
- **Focus employees must inform personnel managers prior to the week** they are requesting comp time for. Personnel managers will relay the request to Focus executives (CEO/CTO/CPO).
- **Focus executives are the sole authorities for approving comp time.**

### Requesting Comp Time

- Inform your personnel manager in writing (Slack, email, etc.) of your intent to work more than 44 hours in a week before that week begins. Include the client's request in that message, along with your estimate of how many hours you anticipate working.
  - When additional work cannot be reasonably anticipated before the week has begun, seek approval as soon as the need arises. Retroactive approval is not guaranteed.
- Your personnel manager will relay the message to the appropriate Focus executive, who will approve or deny the request.
- Your personnel manager will inform you of the decision and any stipulations (for example, no more than 48 hours in a week).
  - If your comp time request is denied, inform your project team and the client and limit yourself to 40 hours of project work that week.
- If approved, enter your hours worked as normal in your timesheet.
- After your billing period has ended, you have submitted your timesheet, and your timesheet has been approved, let your personnel manager know how many hours over a full load you worked (hours beyond 8 hrs/day × days in the billing period).
  - Once you meet the eligibility threshold, comp time is granted at a 1:1 rate for eligible hours worked. For example, 44 hours in a week → 4 hours of comp time, 45 hours → 5 hours, and so on.
- Your personnel manager will share your request with HR (cc'ing you and the relevant executive).
- HR will grant you comp time in Rippling.

### Using Comp Time

- Comp time appears as a category in Rippling, so you can use it when requesting time off if you have comp time available.
- Comp time accrual is capped at 80 hours. If you have accrued 80 hours of comp time, you must use comp time before you can accrue any more. There is no limit to the cumulative amount of comp time you accrue over any given period of time.
- Accrued comp time expires at the end of each calendar year and is not paid out upon separation from Focus, except where payout is required by applicable state law.

## Time Tracking & Documentation

### All Employees

- **Daily time tracking:** Accurately track all time worked using the company's designated time tracking system
- **Project code assignment:** Allocate time to appropriate client contracts and internal project codes
- **Accuracy requirement:** Time entries must be completed within 24 hours of work performed

See [Focus Time Tracking Policies](technology.md) for full guidance on the time tracking system.

### Government Contract Compliance

- **Billable hour documentation:** All billable hours must include detailed task descriptions sufficient for client invoicing and audit purposes
- **Rate category accuracy:** Time must be logged under the correct labor category and rate as defined in the applicable contract
- **Overhead vs. direct:** Clear delineation between direct client work and overhead activities

## Client Site Work & Travel

When client contracts require on-site work, standard work week expectations apply unless modified by client requirements. Travel time to and from client sites is compensated according to applicable labor laws and contract terms. Client-mandated extended hours must be pre-approved and documented.

For full travel reimbursement procedures and rates, see the [Travel Policy](travel.md).

## Performance & Accountability

Supervisors will conduct regular one-on-one meetings to assess workload and work-life balance. Extended work patterns will be reviewed periodically to identify improvement opportunities. Employees are encouraged to raise work-life balance concerns with their supervisor or People Operations at any time.

## Exceptions & Special Circumstances

### Contract-Specific Requirements

Some government contracts may impose specific work hour requirements that supersede this policy. Contract terms take precedence, and employees will be notified of special requirements during contract onboarding.

### Emergency Response

During government-declared emergencies or critical system failures, standard approval processes may be expedited. All applicable overtime and compensation rules remain in effect, and post-emergency recovery time will be provided when operationally feasible.

## Policy Compliance & Enforcement

### Manager Responsibilities

- Approve overtime only when justified.
- Regularly monitor employee work hours and well-being
- Maintain proper records of all extended work approvals and justifications
- Clearly communicate expectations and deadlines in advance

### Employee Responsibilities

- Report time honestly and accurately
- Communicate potential schedule conflicts or concerns in advance
- Balance client needs with personal well-being using professional judgment
- Adhere to all aspects of this policy

> **Time Reporting Violations:** Inaccurate time reporting may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination. Managers who consistently fail to properly manage work hour approvals may also face corrective action.

## Independent Contractors & 1099 Consultants

Contractors work according to deliverable-based or hourly parameters defined in their Statement of Work (SOW). For hourly engagements, typical weekly commitments range from 20–40 hours as specified in the contract. Contractors maintain scheduling autonomy while remaining reasonably available during core collaboration hours for client meetings and team coordination.

> **No Expectation of Uncompensated Overtime:** Unlike W-2 exempt employees, contractors are never expected to work beyond their contracted scope without additional compensation. If project demands require additional hours, a formal SOW amendment or change order is required. Contractors have full discretion to accept or decline requests for additional work.

### Scope Management for Contractors

- All hours worked must be compensated according to the consulting agreement terms
- Extended work periods (proposals, go-live events) require advance negotiation and may warrant premium compensation
- Immediately notify your Focus project manager if workload consistently exceeds contracted parameters
- Consistent overages signal scope creep requiring SOW clarification or amendment

### Contractor Time Tracking

- Track all billable hours accurately using designated systems
- Assign time to appropriate project codes and client contracts
- Adhere to any maximum weekly or monthly hour caps specified in the SOW
- Submit timesheets according to the agreed-upon schedule (typically weekly or bi-weekly)
- Log time under correct labor categories with documentation sufficient for government audit

Questions about work schedules, scope, or hour limits? Contact your assigned Contact Lead or the Business Operations team for SOW modifications.
