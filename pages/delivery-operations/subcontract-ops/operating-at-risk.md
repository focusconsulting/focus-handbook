# SOP: Operating at Risk

_What Focus does when a prime contractor is performing work before a contract amendment or authorization to proceed has been formally executed._
**Scope** All prime subcontracts (Nava, eSimplicity, Accenture, and others) · **Version** 1.0 · **Owners** Delivery Lead, CDO

**Key principle** The first move is always to request an Authority to Proceed. Time and dollar thresholds only apply when the prime cannot or will not issue one.

## Purpose

Establish how Focus evaluates, documents, and limits its exposure when a prime contractor asks Focus staff to continue work — or when work has already been underway — without a formally executed subcontract amendment or authorization to proceed (ATP). This situation arises when prime contractors are themselves operating at risk while awaiting a client's signature on a contract modification or option year exercise.

## Background

In federal and state contracting, "operating at risk" means performing work before the contract vehicle authorizing that work has been fully executed. The prime takes on risk because if the modification is ultimately not executed, they may not be able to bill for the work. When a prime asks Focus to continue working in this environment, Focus is taking on a downstream version of the same risk — performing work that may not be billable if the amendment falls through.

**Example (Massachusetts PFML, June 2026):** Nava notified Focus that it was operating at risk while awaiting executed amendments from the client. Nava indicated it could not issue the Focus subcontract amendment or ATP until the client returned the executed document. Nava's program manager separately confirmed that work would continue and that the amendment, once executed, would cover the at-risk period.

This SOP governs how Focus handles these situations: when to continue, when to pause, how to protect staff, and what to document.

## Contracts Team on All Prime Communications

All written communications with primes about an at-risk period — including the initial notification response, written assurance requests, and any decision to pause or continue — must CC **contracts@focusconsulting.io**. This applies from the first response.

## Authority to Proceed: First Ask, Every Time

Whenever a prime notifies Focus that it is operating at risk, Focus's immediate response is to request a formal **Authority to Proceed (ATP)** from the prime's contracts team. An ATP is a written instrument — issued by the prime — that authorizes Focus to perform specific work during a defined period, even when the underlying subcontract amendment has not yet been executed. It gives Focus documented authorization to bill for that work once the amendment is executed.

The ATP request goes to the prime contracts team (not the program manager) and should specify:

- The scope of work Focus is being asked to perform during the at-risk period
- The period of performance the ATP covers
- Confirmation that the work will be billable and paid upon amendment execution

If the prime issues an ATP, Focus can proceed with confidence. The at-risk log still applies, and the Delivery Lead still monitors for execution, but the time and dollar thresholds below are not the primary constraint when an ATP is in hand.

**The thresholds below apply only when the prime cannot or will not issue an ATP.** That is when Focus's exposure is unprotected and the limits become enforceable.

## Thresholds: When Focus Will Pause (ATP Not Issued)

Focus's default when a prime provides written assurance and a credible timeline but cannot issue an ATP is to continue work within defined limits. If either threshold below is reached without a formally executed amendment or ATP, the CDO makes the call on whether to pause:

| Threshold | Default Limit | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time at risk | 30 calendar days | Measured from the date Focus is first notified it is operating at risk |
| Dollar value at risk | One month of the affected staff's contract value (to be set per engagement) | Estimate based on billing rates × FTE count × days at risk |

These thresholds are recommendations. The CDO may adjust them based on the prime relationship, program criticality, and the credibility of the assurance received. Any adjustment must be documented.

"Pause" does not necessarily mean staff stop working immediately. It means the CDO and Delivery Lead assess options: reducing hours, shifting to non-billable activity, or a full stop. The right answer depends on what's operationally feasible and what the prime has committed to in writing.

## Process Steps

### Step 1: Identify and Document the At-Risk Situation

When a Delivery Lead or CDO learns that Focus may be operating at risk — from a prime notification, a missed amendment date, or a program manager's statement — they immediately document:

- Date Focus was notified (or date the at-risk period began, if earlier)
- The source of notification (email, Slack, call) and who communicated it
- What was said: the prime's characterization of the situation, any stated timeline for execution, and any commitment made regarding continued work
- The specific amendment or ATP that is pending
- The staff affected and their estimated burn rate

Save all communications (email screenshots, Slack exports) to a designated folder in Focus's document management system, labeled by contract and date.

### Step 2: Request an Authority to Proceed

Before continuing work beyond a brief bridging period (five business days), the Delivery Lead — CCing contracts@focusconsulting.io — formally requests an **ATP** from the prime's contracts team. The request should be specific: scope, period of performance, and confirmation that work performed under the ATP will be billable once the amendment executes.

If the prime issues an ATP, document it in the at-risk log and proceed. Continue monitoring for amendment execution, but the thresholds in this SOP are not the binding constraint while a valid ATP is in place.

If the prime cannot issue an ATP, the Delivery Lead requests written confirmation from the prime's contracts team — not just the program manager — that:

- The prime intends to execute the amendment covering Focus's work
- The at-risk period will be billable and paid once executed
- The anticipated execution timeline

A statement from the program manager alone is useful context but is not sufficient. Only the prime contracts team has authority to commit on billing and payment. With no ATP and no contracts-team confirmation, the time and dollar thresholds in this SOP are active from that point forward.

### Step 3: Notify Affected Staff

Affected Focus team members are told:

- That their contract amendment is pending and has not yet been executed
- That Focus has received assurance from the prime that work will continue and that the at-risk period will be covered once executed
- What Focus's plan is if the amendment is not executed (see Step 5)
- That their pay will not be interrupted during the at-risk period, regardless of amendment status — Focus assumes that obligation internally until the situation is resolved

Do not leave staff in the dark about their status. The uncertainty of an at-risk period is stressful; the way to manage that is transparency about what Focus knows and what it is doing about it.

### Step 4: Monitor Against Thresholds

The Delivery Lead tracks the at-risk period against the 30-day / one-month-billing thresholds. If the execution timeline slips, the Delivery Lead:

- Re-contacts the prime contracts team to request an updated timeline in writing
- Notifies the CDO of the slip
- Reassesses whether Focus should adjust the work scope or hours to limit further exposure

Each re-contact and response is documented in the at-risk log.

### Step 5: Escalate to CDO at Threshold

When either threshold (30 days or one month of contract value) is reached without a formally executed amendment, the Delivery Lead escalates to the CDO, who makes one of three calls:

- **Continue with conditions.** The CDO negotiates additional written protections with the prime — e.g., a letter of intent, an interim ATP, or a written commitment from the contracting officer — and sets a new threshold.
- **Reduce scope.** Focus limits the work to a maintainable minimum (e.g., one part-time FTE) while execution is pending, reducing further exposure while preserving the program relationship.
- **Pause.** Focus staff stop billing. The CDO notifies the prime that Focus cannot continue at risk beyond the current threshold, and communicates clearly what it would take to resume (executed amendment or ATP).

A pause is not a breach of the subcontract — Focus is not obligated to work without authorization. The CDO handles the prime-facing conversation directly.

### Step 6: Document the Resolution

Once the amendment is executed (or the program ends at risk), the Delivery Lead:

- Confirms the at-risk period is covered under the executed amendment
- Updates the at-risk log with the resolution date and outcome
- Confirms with the CDO whether any internal adjustments are needed (e.g., payroll timing, invoice sequencing)
- Notifies affected staff that the amendment has been executed

If the amendment is never executed and Focus has performed work at risk that will not be paid, the CDO and legal counsel determine how to proceed.

## What Focus Will Not Do

- Continue operating at risk indefinitely because a prime or program manager has verbally indicated that things will work out
- Allow staff to self-manage their own at-risk situation — if a team member hears informally that their amendment is delayed, that surfaces to the Delivery Lead immediately
- Treat a program-manager assurance as equivalent to a contracts-team commitment on billing and payment
- Interrupt pay to Focus staff during an at-risk period before a formal decision to pause has been made
