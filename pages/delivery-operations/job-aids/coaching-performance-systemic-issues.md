# Job Aid 01: Coaching vs. Performance vs. Systemic Issues

When a team member is struggling, the first question should not be **“Is this a performance problem?”**

The first question is:

> **What is preventing this person from consistently meeting the expectation?**

A missed commitment, communication breakdown, quality concern, or lack of ownership can have very different root causes. Before escalating an issue, determine whether you are dealing with a **coaching issue, performance issue, or systemic issue**.

## The Three Categories

| Category | What it means | Manager's responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Coaching Issue** | The person needs additional skill, clarity, feedback, experience, or support to consistently meet the expectation. | Teach, clarify, demonstrate, provide feedback, and create opportunities to practice. |
| **Performance Issue** | The expectation is reasonable and understood, the person has the capability and support to meet it, but the gap continues despite feedback and coaching. | Establish accountability, document the recurring gap, set explicit improvement expectations, and follow Focus's [performance-management process](../../performance.md) when appropriate. |
| **Systemic Issue** | The environment, process, workload, dependencies, leadership decisions, or team structure are preventing reliable performance. | Fix the system rather than treating the individual as the problem. |

## The Diagnostic

When you observe a delivery concern, work through these questions **before categorizing it as a performance issue**:

| Ask | If NO... | If YES... |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Is the expectation clear and appropriate for this person's role and level?** | Clarify the expectation. | Continue. |
| **2. Is the person reasonably in control of the outcome?** | Investigate dependencies/system constraints. | Continue. |
| **3. Does the person have the knowledge and skill to meet the expectation?** | Coach/develop. | Continue. |
| **4. Has the person received specific feedback about the gap?** | Give feedback. | Continue. |
| **5. Have reasonable support, resources, and time been provided?** | Provide support. | Continue. |
| **6. Does the same gap continue after expectations, feedback, and support are clear?** | Continue coaching/observation. | This may now be a **performance issue**. |

A useful shorthand is:

**CAN'T → Coaching**

The person does not yet have the skill, experience, clarity, or judgment needed to consistently meet the expectation.

**WON'T / ISN'T → Performance**

The person understands the expectation, has the capability and reasonable support to meet it, has received feedback, and the behavior or outcome continues.

**CAN'T BECAUSE → Systemic**

Something in the team's environment is preventing success.

## Evidence Before Labels

Retro comments, stakeholder feedback, missed sprint commitments, defects, escalations, or concerns raised by another lead should be treated as **signals**, not conclusions.

Before having a performance conversation, the manager should be able to answer:

1. **What specifically happened?**
2. **What was expected instead?**
3. **Was that expectation previously clear?**
4. **What impact did the gap have?**
5. **What feedback or coaching has already been provided?**
6. **Has the behavior occurred repeatedly?**

Avoid labels such as:

> “You aren't taking ownership.”

Instead, describe observable behavior and the expected behavior:

> “On the last two deliverables, I had to follow up several times to understand status and next steps. At your level, I expect you to proactively communicate progress, surface blockers, and close the loop without repeated prompting.”

Specific behavior can be coached. Vague character judgments cannot.

## Common Scenarios

| Scenario | Start Here | What to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Team member requires repeated follow-ups | **Coaching** | Do they understand expectations for follow-through, prioritization, and closing loops? |
| Progress isn't visible to the team or lead | **Coaching / Systemic** | Is work actually stalled, or is progress simply not being communicated? |
| Senior team member isn't demonstrating expected ownership | **Coaching / Role Clarity** | Have ownership expectations for their level been explicitly established? |
| Decisions are difficult to understand or poorly explained | **Coaching** | Can they clearly communicate the recommendation, rationale, tradeoffs, and next action? |
| Team member repeatedly surfaces problems without helping move them forward | **Coaching** | Do they know when they are expected to recommend an action, facilitate resolution, or escalate? |
| Team member reports that work is on track, declines support, then repeatedly misses commitments | **Potential Performance Issue** | Were risks knowable, expectations clear, and support available? Is this recurring after feedback? |
| Several team members are struggling with the same issue | **Systemic** | Look at workload, priorities, dependencies, process, leadership, tooling, and role clarity. |
| Individual has too many competing priorities | **Systemic first** | Who established the priorities? What should be stopped or deprioritized? |
| Team receives conflicting direction from leaders | **Systemic** | Leadership must align priorities and decision rights before evaluating individual execution. |
| Team member struggles with a new responsibility they've never performed before | **Coaching** | Determine what training, modeling, pairing, or feedback they need. |

## A Missed Commitment Is Not Automatically a Performance Issue

In delivery environments, commitments move. Requirements change. Dependencies fail. Stakeholders introduce new priorities. Technical complexity emerges.

The manager should therefore investigate **how the individual managed the commitment**, not merely whether the date was missed.

There is a meaningful difference between:

> “We're at risk of missing Friday because the dependency hasn't been resolved. I've escalated it, and here are our options.”

and:

> “Everything is fine.”

followed by an unexpected miss.

The second situation may indicate a reliability or judgment concern because the team lost the opportunity to manage the risk.

## Expectations for Senior Team Members and Leads

As responsibility increases, Focus expects greater independence in creating clarity and moving work forward.

Senior team members should increasingly be able to:

- **Own the outcome**, not simply complete assigned tasks.
- **Surface risk early**, while the team still has options.
- **Close the loop** without repeated prompting.
- **Make thinking visible** by clearly explaining decisions and tradeoffs.
- **Move problems forward**, even when they do not personally know the solution.
- **Protect team focus** by identifying competing priorities and escalating capacity constraints.

This does **not** mean leaders must always arrive with a solution.

A healthy escalation can be:

> “I don't know the solution yet. Here's what I think is happening, here's what I've tried, and here's where I need help.”

The expectation is not **“Never bring me a problem.”**

The expectation is **“Help move the problem forward.”**

## Moving From Coaching to Performance Management

A coaching issue should not remain in an informal coaching loop indefinitely.

Consider transitioning into formal performance management when:

**Expectation is clear → Capability exists → Feedback has been given → Support has been provided → Reasonable time has passed → The same material gap continues**

At that point, the conversation changes from:

> **Coaching:** “Let's work on helping you become more effective at this.”

to:

> **Performance:** “This is an expectation of your role, we've discussed the gap and provided support, and the required improvement has not occurred consistently.”

People Managers should involve the appropriate Focus leadership/People Operations before beginning formal performance action.

## Scrum Metrics Are Signals, Not Individual Scorecards

Do not use **velocity, story points completed, ticket counts, PR counts, bugs, or similar Scrum metrics** as standalone measures of individual performance.

They can help identify where investigation is needed, but they rarely explain **why** something is happening.

Evaluate people more holistically through:

**Reliability • Quality • Ownership • Collaboration • Judgment • Communication • Role Competency • Impact**

The goal is not to create a delivery organization where everyone learns how to make their metrics look green.

The goal is to create teams where **problems become visible early enough to solve them.**
