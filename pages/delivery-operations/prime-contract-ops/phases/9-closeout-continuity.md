# Phase 9: Closeout & Continuity

_Part of the [Prime Contract Lifecycle](../index.md). Focus-prime contracts only._

**Trigger:** Roughly six months before the contract ends or changes.
**Owner:** The program manager, with the CDO on the client-facing exit.
**Ends:** The lifecycle. Lessons captured here feed the next prime's [Phase 0](0-capture-kp-notification.md).

## Why this phase matters

Contracts end, on schedule or otherwise, and how Focus leaves matters as much as how it arrived. A contract that ends on schedule, isn't recompeted, or is lost on recompete all get the same discipline: a runway long enough to move our people well, hand the client what they need to keep running, and protect the relationship for whatever comes next. Losing a recompete is never a reason to burn a relationship; we may bid again or work with the client elsewhere.

## What happens

Give the wind-down roughly a six-month runway.

- **Continuity planning for our people.** Practice leads and the program manager identify where staff can move next, well before the last week. Six months is when the thinking starts; Focus's 60-day redeployment and 90-day reduction notice windows are the formal-notice floor, not the trigger.
- **What the client needs to keep running.** Produce the training, SOPs, and documentation that let the client (or an incoming contractor) operate after we leave. Under most contracts, processes and procedures are intellectual property that stay with the contract, not with Focus.
- **Preserve the relationship.** The CDO manages the client-facing exit conversation with an eye on future business, regardless of why the contract is ending.
- **Knowledge transfer to whoever comes next.** If another company is taking over, transfer knowledge and give them a real onboarding, the way we would want an incumbent to do for us. Federal continuity-of-services terms (FAR 52.237-3) can require up to 90 days of phase-in/phase-out support, so plan for it rather than being surprised by it ([Acquisition.gov](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.237-3)).
- **Asset and access offboarding.** Close out Focus systems, accounts, and access tied to the contract, and deliver anything owed to the client under the contract terms.
- **Closeout retrospective.** Capture what worked and what didn't over the life of the contract, for the next prime and for a possible future recompete.

## Principles we're applying

- **Leave well.** A clean exit is part of the client relationship, one of the firm's two core assets (David Maister, [_Managing the Professional Service Firm_](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Managing-The-Professional-Service-Firm/David-H-Maister/9780684834313)).
- **Knowledge transfer is what provides continuity.** Documentation, formal training, and shadowing are how the work survives the handoff ([RELI Group](https://www.religroupinc.com/news-insights/navigating-the-transition-best-practices-for-government-contract-teams/)).
- **Every closeout feeds the next capture.** The retrospective is an input to the next prime, closing the lifecycle loop.

## Done when

- Outgoing staff have a redeployment plan set before the end date.
- The client has the documentation, SOPs, and training to keep running.
- Knowledge transfer to the successor (if any) is complete, and access and assets are offboarded.
- A closeout retrospective is captured for future BD and delivery.
