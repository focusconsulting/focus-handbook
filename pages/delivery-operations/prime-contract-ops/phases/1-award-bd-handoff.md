# Phase 1: Award & BD-to-Delivery Handoff

_Part of the [Prime Contract Lifecycle](../index.md). Focus-prime contracts only._

**Trigger:** Notice of award.
**Owner:** BD, handing off to the assigned key personnel.
**Hands off to:** [Phase 2: Staffing & Recruiting](2-staffing-recruiting.md).

## Why this phase matters

Winning the contract triggers the prime lifecycle. The proposal that won carries a set of assumptions and a staffing plan that the delivery team didn't write. Before anyone commits to executing it, the people who will deliver need to understand what was promised, on what basis, and where it stops matching reality. This is the cheapest moment to catch a gap between the bid and what's deliverable, and it's the moment most primes skip.

## What happens

BD runs a working session that walks the assigned key personnel through:

- The **market research** that informed the bid: who the client is, what they said they wanted, what the competitive landscape looked like.
- The **proposal** itself: what Focus committed to.
- The **bid assumptions**: staffing levels, rates, timelines, and dependencies the price and schedule rest on.
- The **proposed staffing plan**: who the bid named and in what roles.

This is also the first real conversation about how the team wants to approach the work and how it wants to operate. Surface promise-versus-reality gaps here, and seed them into the risk register that Phase 3 will stand up.

## Principles we're applying

- **Understand the engagement before you run it.** Every professional service engagement rests on an economic and delivery model; managing it well starts with knowing that model, not discovering it mid-delivery (David Maister, [_Managing the Professional Service Firm_](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Managing-The-Professional-Service-Firm/David-H-Maister/9780684834313)).
- **Build shared context deliberately.** The handoff exists so delivery inherits the reasoning, not just the deliverable.
- A structured post-award orientation is standard practice precisely because it lets duties transfer cleanly from the people who won the work to the people who will do it.

## Done when

- The key personnel can state what was promised, at what price and schedule, and on what assumptions.
- Every promise-versus-reality gap is written down and assigned an owner.
- The staffing plan is confirmed or explicitly flagged for rework in Phase 2.
