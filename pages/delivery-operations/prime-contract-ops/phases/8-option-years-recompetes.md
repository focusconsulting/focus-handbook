# Phase 8: Option Years & Recompetes

_Part of the [Prime Contract Lifecycle](../index.md). Focus-prime contracts only._

**Trigger:** The option-year and recompete windows.
**Owner:** The program manager who owns the account, with the CDO on contracts.
**Hands off to:** [Phase 9: Closeout & Continuity](9-closeout-continuity.md) (only if the work ends).

## Why this phase matters

Option years and recompetes are delivery-led growth, and they belong to the account-owning program manager. This is not work that gets handed back to BD or an executive once delivery is underway; it's part of the PM's job and part of how the PM's success is measured. Keeping the business healthy and moving forward is the point of putting an account owner on the contract in the first place.

The stakes are real. Incumbents win roughly 70 to 80 percent of recompetes, but the ones who lose usually lose to complacency: weak CPARS, a relationship that lived in one or two contacts, or a proposal that coasted on "they know us" ([Summit Strategy](https://www.summitstrategywins.com/blog-posts/the-recompete-trap-why-incumbents-lose)). Prioritizing the customer relationship measurably raises win rates ([Hi-Q Group](https://hi-qgroup.com/how-to-win-recompete-govcon/)).

## What happens

- **Option years: start at least six months out.** Check whether the contract is on track, whether the client intends to exercise, and whether anything needs attention before the decision point. The account manager drives the readiness check and the client conversation; the CDO supports with the contracting officer to confirm intent to exercise.
- **Recompetes: start a full year out.** A recompete needs a strategy a year in advance. The account manager owns it, drawing on the lessons and relationship context accumulated during delivery: client intimacy, on-site visits, and a real read on agency priorities. The CDO and BD support on shaping and pursuit.
- **The case to plan for.** When a contract is in an option year and a recompete may land within roughly a year, recompete work should already be underway: understanding agency strategy so we can position for a sole-source continuation or, if it's competed, help shape the solicitation.

## Principles we're applying

- **Delivery-led growth is the account owner's mandate,** and it's how the program manager is evaluated.
- **Don't let the relationship concentrate.** Contacts move, retire, and get reassigned; a broad relationship map is insurance against the recompete surprise.
- **Rebuild the strategy, don't roll it forward.** The pricing and approach that won the original award are rarely what wins the recompete ([Summit Strategy](https://www.summitstrategywins.com/blog-posts/the-recompete-trap-why-incumbents-lose)).

## Done when

- For an option year: intent to exercise is confirmed and the team is prepped for continuation or change.
- For a recompete: a strategy exists a year out and is being executed, with the customer relationship and past performance actively managed.
