# Phase 3: Transition Planning & Internal Pre-Kickoff

_Part of the [Prime Contract Lifecycle](../index.md). Focus-prime contracts only._

**Trigger:** Seats are filled.
**Owner:** The contract's delivery lead, ideally a program manager.
**Hands off to:** [Phase 4: Internal Kickoff & Team Formation](4-internal-kickoff-team-formation.md).

## Why this phase matters

This is where Focus decides how it will actually work on the contract, before we ever sit down with the client. Skip it and the team discovers in week one that no one has system access, the government furnished equipment hasn't been requested, and a deliverable is due in three weeks that nobody scoped. Good transition planning starts as soon as the contract is awarded, which is what makes a phase-in feel deliberate instead of reactive ([RELI Group](https://www.religroupinc.com/news-insights/navigating-the-transition-best-practices-for-government-contract-teams/)).

Focus is moving to a model where a dedicated **program manager** leads delivery on prime contracts, and we now hire for that role rather than stretching a practitioner into it. That PM owns transition planning and runs the internal pre-kickoff.

## What happens

The delivery lead runs an internal pre-kickoff that covers:

- **Transition cadence.** A defined meeting rhythm from now to the start date.
- **Contract distribution.** Get the contract, PWS/SOW, and any special clauses into the hands of the leads who will manage scope day to day, not just BD and the CDO.
- **Risk management.** Stand up the program risk register, seeded with the gaps surfaced at the BD handoff and during staffing.
- **System access and GFE.** Confirm client-system access is provisioned and Focus-side systems are set up under the correct contract code. Where the contract uses government furnished equipment, confirm what's provided, who requests it, and the lead time, so no one is waiting on a laptop on day one.
- **First-90-day deliverables.** Identify the deliverables the contract requires in the first 90 days and start a concrete plan to hit them. Executing that plan is [Phase 6](6-first-90-day-plan.md); this is where we name what's due.
- **Knowledge transfer off current assignments.** The outgoing team completes documentation and KT on their current work before shifting, so they arrive fully present instead of half-tied to the old contract.

## Principles we're applying

- **A named owner for the transition.** The program manager owns this end to end; transition is a role, not a shared afterthought.
- **Engagement setup is management, not paperwork.** In a professional service firm, disciplined engagement management is how quality and margin are protected (David Maister, [_Managing the Professional Service Firm_](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Managing-The-Professional-Service-Firm/David-H-Maister/9780684834313)).
- **Start at award.** The runway between award and start is the transition's whole budget; spend it on purpose.

## Done when

- The internal pre-kickoff checklist is complete and the risk register is live.
- Access and GFE are requested with lead times understood.
- First-90-day deliverables are named and owned.
- KT off current assignments is scheduled or done.
