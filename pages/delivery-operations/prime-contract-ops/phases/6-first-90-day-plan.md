# Phase 6: First 90-Day Plan

_Part of the [Prime Contract Lifecycle](../index.md). Focus-prime contracts only._

**Trigger:** Client kickoff done.
**Owner:** The delivery lead / program manager, with the contract leads.
**Hands off to:** [Phase 7: Steady-State Delivery](7-steady-state-delivery.md).

## Why this phase matters

The first 90 days are the most critical stretch of a new contract, and they run on their own plan rather than dropping straight into steady-state autopilot. This is when Focus builds the working relationships and earns the credibility that everything later draws on. Early wins are how a new leader builds momentum and a credit line for bigger bets later; pick three or four places you know you can deliver and deliver them (Michael Watkins, [_The First 90 Days_](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/15824358-the-first-90-days)). How these 90 days go shapes how the client judges Focus for the rest of the contract, and eventually the recompete.

## What happens

- **Map the stakeholder ecosystem.** Identify who holds influence, who signs off, who the day-to-day contacts are, and who the quiet skeptics are. Don't let the relationship concentrate in one or two people; contacts move, retire, and get reassigned, and a relationship that lived in one inbox leaves with them.
- **Build the relationships.** Invest in the client contacts deliberately in these weeks, while attention is high and no crisis has happened yet.
- **Hit the early deliverables.** Prime contracts usually have deliverables due inside the first 30 and 60 days. Name them, own the dates, and work the concrete plan started in [Phase 3](3-transition-planning.md). These early deliverables are the first, most visible early wins.
- **Never surprise the client with bad news.** If something is slipping, the client hears it from us first, with a plan attached.

## Principles we're applying

- **Secure early wins.** Visible, meaningful results early prove competence and build the credibility that later, riskier work depends on ([The First 90 Days](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/15824358-the-first-90-days)).
- **Diagnose the situation you actually inherited** before acting at scale; a startup contract is not a turnaround, and the plan should match (Watkins' STARS framing).
- **Relationships are one of the firm's two core assets.** The first 90 days are when we start building that asset for this account (David Maister, [_Managing the Professional Service Firm_](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Managing-The-Professional-Service-Firm/David-H-Maister/9780684834313)).

## Done when

- The stakeholder ecosystem is mapped and relationships are underway, not concentrated in one contact.
- Every 30- and 60-day deliverable has been met or actively managed with the client informed.
- The team has a short list of delivered early wins to point to.
