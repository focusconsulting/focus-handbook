# Phase 2: Staffing & Recruiting

_Part of the [Prime Contract Lifecycle](../index.md). Focus-prime contracts only._

**Trigger:** BD-to-delivery handoff complete.
**Owner:** Practice lead(s) with the Chief Delivery Officer, run as a formal kickoff meeting.
**Hands off to:** [Phase 3: Transition Planning & Internal Pre-Kickoff](3-transition-planning.md).

## Why this phase matters

Staffing a prime is the single most important thing we do, and it's where our hardest lessons live. We have moved people into new roles without setting them up to succeed: no job description for the new role, no honest check of whether the person could carry it out. That's the mistake this phase exists to stop. Being a Focus employee is not the same as being right for the seat. In a professional service firm, the two assets that matter are client relationships and the firm's stock of skill and talent; staffing is where we spend the second one, so we spend it deliberately (David Maister, [_Managing the Professional Service Firm_](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Managing-The-Professional-Service-Firm/David-H-Maister/9780684834313)).

## What happens

Run this as a kickoff meeting, not a hallway decision. The practice lead(s) meet with the CDO and whoever else the seat requires, even when the intended person is already a Focus employee.

- **Assess the bench, decide where to hire.** Measure current bench strength against what the contract requires, and decide which seats we fill internally and which we recruit for.
- **Evaluate everyone against the role's competencies.** Current employees and outside candidates alike are measured against what the role actually needs: mission fit, technical capability, consulting know-how, and AI fluency, not just a technical skill match. Someone on a subcontract today whom we hoped to move into a KP or lead slot may, on evaluation, not meet the role. When that happens, we hire outside. Say it plainly and early.
- **Write the role down.** Anyone changing roles gets a job description written for the new role and the support to carry it out.
- **Start clearances now.** Kick off any required security clearances immediately. Federal adjudications run months (a Top Secret adjudication has averaged around 144 days), so clearance timelines drive start dates more than almost anything else. Where a cleared seat is at risk, line up a letter of intent or teaming arrangement as a backstop.

## Principles we're applying

- **Right people in the right seats** is the second lifecycle principle. Evaluate against the role's defined competencies, not against who happens to be available. This is the role-scorecard discipline from _Scaling People_ ([Stripe Press](https://press.stripe.com/scaling-people)): decide what "great" looks like for the seat first, then measure every candidate against it.
- **Set people up to succeed before they start**, not after they're struggling.
- **Protect Day-1 readiness.** The goal is to have key personnel in place and ready on the contract's first day, which is why clearances and backstops start now.

## Done when

- Every seat has a named owner who was evaluated against the role, internal or external.
- New and changed roles have written job descriptions.
- Required clearances are initiated and their lead times are reflected in the start-date plan.
