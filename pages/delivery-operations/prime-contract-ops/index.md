# Prime Contracts

_How Focus operates on engagements where it holds the prime contract._

On prime engagements, the general [Compliance](../compliance.md) baseline applies to everyone. As the prime, Focus also carries obligations a subcontractor does not: the direct client relationship, subcontractor oversight, and reporting to the contracting officer. This page maps the full lifecycle of a Focus prime contract, from the moment we name someone key personnel in a bid through closeout. Each phase links to its own page with the full guidance. The [CDO Engagement Model](cdo-engagement-model.md) carries the operating detail (meeting cadence, escalation, FAQs) that sits underneath it.

We are writing this down now because we are entering a new phase. Focus is winning and running its own prime work, and the way we staff and stand up a prime decides whether it succeeds. Most of our hard lessons come from moving people onto new work without setting them up to do it well. The framework below is built to stop that from happening again.

## Two principles that run through the whole lifecycle

**Notify early.** The moment a person is named key personnel on a bid, other people's plans depend on it: the program they're on today, the leads who'll have to backfill them, the client who may lose them. We tell those people as early as we can, not when the transition is already on top of us. Early notice is what makes transition planning, backfill, and restaffing possible instead of reactive. This starts in BD, before we've even won.

**Right people in the right seats.** Staffing a prime is the single most important thing we do, and being a Focus employee is not the same as being right for the seat. Every person considered for a key-personnel or lead role, current employees included, gets evaluated against the competencies the role actually requires. Sometimes that means someone we hoped to move into a lead slot doesn't meet the role, and we hire outside instead. We would rather have that conversation before the contract starts than manage the consequences after. (This mirrors the role-scorecard discipline in Claire Hughes Johnson's _Scaling People_: hire and place against the role's defined competencies, not against who is available.)

## Prime Contract Lifecycle

The lifecycle has ten phases. The first begins during capture, before award; the last begins six months before the contract ends. Ownership shifts as the contract matures: the CDO and contract leads stand it up together, and once it's running, the contract leads (the program manager who owns the account) own it through delivery, growth, and wind-down. Each summary below links to the full phase guide.

**Stand-up: the CDO and contract leads own it together.**

### 0. Capture & Key-Personnel Notification (BD)

_Owner: BD, with the affected program leads._ When BD names its intended key personnel, it notifies the program leads those people work for today, so transition and backfill start early, before the win. **[Full guide →](phases/0-capture-kp-notification.md)**

### 1. Award & BD-to-Delivery Handoff

_Owner: BD, handing off to the assigned key personnel._ At notice of award, BD walks the key personnel through the market research, proposal, bid assumptions, and staffing plan, and surfaces promise-versus-reality gaps. **[Full guide →](phases/1-award-bd-handoff.md)**

### 2. Staffing & Recruiting

_Owner: practice lead(s) with the CDO, run as a kickoff._ Assess the bench, evaluate everyone against the role's competencies, hire outside when an internal person doesn't fit, and start clearances early. **[Full guide →](phases/2-staffing-recruiting.md)**

### 3. Transition Planning & Internal Pre-Kickoff

_Owner: the delivery lead, ideally a program manager._ The PM runs an internal pre-kickoff: transition cadence, contract distribution, risk register, system access and GFE, first-90-day deliverables, and knowledge transfer off current work. **[Full guide →](phases/3-transition-planning.md)**

### 4. Internal Kickoff & Team Formation

_Owner: contract leads and key personnel._ Before we meet the client, the team forms: decision rights (RACI or DACI), the delivery methodology, and the AI-and-tooling question, settled up front. **[Full guide →](phases/4-internal-kickoff-team-formation.md)**

### 5. Client Kickoff

_Owner: contract leads._ The contract leads run the client kickoff, a major milestone. Protect enough runway from award for them to arrive prepared. **[Full guide →](phases/5-client-kickoff.md)**

**Run and wind-down: the contract leads own it. The program manager owns the account and drives delivery-led growth.**

### 6. First 90-Day Plan

_Owner: delivery lead / program manager with contract leads._ The critical ramp: map the stakeholder ecosystem, build relationships, and hit the 30/60/90-day deliverables as visible early wins. **[Full guide →](phases/6-first-90-day-plan.md)**

### 7. Steady-State Delivery

_Owner: the program manager, now the account owner._ Assimilate into the client's Scrum or SAFe cadence. The PM owns delivery and the account; the CDO moves to lighter oversight. **[Full guide →](phases/7-steady-state-delivery.md)**

### 8. Option Years & Recompetes

_Owner: the account-owning program manager, with the CDO on contracts._ Delivery-led growth the PM owns: option years at least six months out, recompete strategy a full year out. **[Full guide →](phases/8-option-years-recompetes.md)**

### 9. Closeout & Continuity

_Owner: the program manager, with the CDO on the client-facing exit._ A six-month runway: redeploy our people, give the client the SOPs and training to keep running, transfer knowledge to the successor, and preserve the relationship. **[Full guide →](phases/9-closeout-continuity.md)**

## Quarterly Program Review

Prime engagements get their own quarterly review, separate from the subcontract portfolio review. Same shape: program status, staffing, risks (technical, contractual, client, and political), and opportunities, with the addition of subcontractor performance and prime-side reporting obligations.
