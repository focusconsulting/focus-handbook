# Practice Onboarding

_How you get oriented to your discipline at Focus: Engineering, Design, or Delivery._

Your practice is the group of people at Focus who share your discipline. Your practice lead is your career manager. They own your performance reviews and professional development, and they set the standards for your discipline. See [Practices](../practices.md) for how the practice structure works and who leads each practice.

## Practice Intro Sessions

Every new hire attends all three practice intro sessions during [cohort week](company.md#cohort-week), not just their own. Our teams are cross-functional, and knowing how the other practices work makes you a better teammate.

| Session | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Engineering** | How we build modern, secure, accessible systems agencies can sustain; engineering standards; tooling; the Focus GitHub org |
| **Delivery** | How we coordinate people, process, and product; our delivery framework; how delivery leads and contract leads support you |
| **Design** | How we approach research and design in government contexts; critique; design tools and standards |

## In Your First Month

- **Meet your practice lead** for an intro conversation about your background, your goals, and how practice check-ins work.
- **Join your practice channel** and your practice's community of practice meetings.
- **Review your three-month plan** with your manager. Plans are tailored by practice. For example, an engineer's plan includes merging ticketed work and doing code reviews, and a designer's plan includes participating in critique.
- **Find a few peers in your discipline** on other projects. Your buddy or practice lead can make introductions.

## In Months 2–3

- **Deeper dive with your practice lead** on standards, frameworks, and what growth looks like in your role (see [Performance & Expectations](../performance.md) and [Compensation & Growth](../compensation.md))
- **Identify a learning goal** or a shadowing opportunity on another engagement
- **Contribute to the practice**, for example by sharing something you learned at a community of practice meeting
