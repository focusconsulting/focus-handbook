# Onboarding

_Your first months at Focus: what to expect, who to connect with, and how to get up to speed._

## How Onboarding Works

Onboarding at Focus happens in three layers. Each one has a different owner and answers a different question.

| Layer | What it answers | Owned by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Company Onboarding](company.md)** | Who is Focus, how do we work, and where do I find things? | Leadership and People Operations |
| **[Practice Onboarding](practice.md)** | What does good look like in my discipline, and who are my peers? | Your practice lead |
| **[Contract Onboarding](contract.md)** | How do I get set up and productive on my client engagement? | Your delivery or contract lead |

Everyone goes through all three. They overlap in your first few weeks, and the [New Hire Checklist](checklist.md) puts them on one timeline so nothing falls between owners.

Every new hire also gets an **[onboarding buddy](buddies.md)**: a teammate outside your reporting line who checks in regularly for your first 90 days and helps with the questions you might not ask your manager.

> **Onboarding Owner:** The onboarding program is managed by the Chief Delivery Officer. Your manager owns your onboarding plan. Your buddy, practice lead, and contract lead each own their part of it.

## What We Believe About Onboarding

A few principles shape how we onboard, several of them drawn from Claire Hughes Johnson's _Scaling People_:

- **The first day and first week set the tone.** You meet your manager 1:1 on day one, and you share a meal with your practice or contract team in week one.
- **Your manager owns your onboarding.** People Operations, practice leads, and buddies all contribute, but your manager is accountable for making sure you have a plan and that it's working.
- **Make the implicit explicit.** We write down how we work, who to ask, and what's expected so you don't have to guess or absorb it slowly.
- **Build relationships early.** Remote work is easier to sustain when you know people across Focus, not only on your project. Cohort week and your buddy exist for this.
- **We ask for feedback and use it.** We ask for your feedback at 30, 60, and 90 days, and we update this section based on what new hires tell us.

## Your First 90 Days at a Glance

| When | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| **Before you start** | Paperwork, accounts, and equipment are ready. Your buddy reaches out. |
| **Day 1** | 1:1 with your manager. Meet your buddy. Confirm access to core tools. |
| **Week 1** | Practice or contract team lunch. Meet your project team. Set up recurring 1:1s. Learn how to log time. |
| **Cohort week** (second week of the month) | Company, practice, and People Operations sessions with your onboarding cohort. |
| **Weeks 2–4** | Contract onboarding, first assignments, weekly buddy check-ins. |
| **Months 2–3** | Contributing independently, building relationships across Focus, and giving feedback on your onboarding. |

### By 30 days

- You understand the project context: who the client is, what the system does, and where the team is in the delivery lifecycle
- You're comfortable with the team's communication norms and meeting cadence
- You can find tools, documentation, and the right person to ask on your own
- You've started on your first tasks with guidance from your lead

### By 90 days

- You're contributing independently with decreasing guidance
- You're part of team rituals (standups, retros, demos, planning)
- You have working relationships beyond your immediate team
- You raise blockers and ask for help early
- You've completed the 90-day onboarding feedback form

> **Introductory Period:** New W2 employees have a 90-day introductory period to demonstrate performance suitability. The company may extend this period if needed. Contractors begin their engagement with a similar 90-day contract-to-hire period before potential conversion to full-time.

## Employment Records

All employees must provide personal information including legal name, address, and contact details during onboarding. Changes to personal information must be reported promptly to People Operations, as they affect tax withholding and benefits eligibility.

The company maintains personnel and payroll records, including your name, address, phone number, insurance information, tax details, and emergency contacts. It is your responsibility to keep this information current.
