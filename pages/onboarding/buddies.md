# Onboarding Buddies

_What an onboarding buddy does, how often to check in, and what's in and out of scope._

Every new hire is paired with an onboarding buddy for their first 90 days. The buddy is a cultural guide and a safe first stop for questions, someone who isn't evaluating the new hire's work.

## Who Can Be a Buddy

- At least 6 months at Focus
- A solid understanding of Focus culture and how we deliver
- Available for regular check-ins in the new hire's first month
- Patient with questions
- **Not** the new hire's manager, and ideally not on the same immediate project team

Buddy work counts toward your contributions in performance reviews (see [Compensation & Growth](../compensation.md)).

## Check-in Cadence

| When | Buddy actions | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Week before start** | Coordinate with the new hire's manager on their plan and Day 1 schedule. Send a welcome note with your contact info. Schedule a Day 1 coffee or lunch. | ~30 min |
| **Day 1** | Morning check-in. Informal coffee or lunch. Confirm they're on the onboarding calendar. | ~1 hr |
| **Week 1** | 30-minute check-in. Walk through Focus tools and Slack channels. Share the unwritten rules. Introduce them to people outside their project. | ~1 hr |
| **Weeks 2–4** | Weekly 30-minute check-ins. Help with first assignments and admin tasks. Talk through the government context (see below). | ~30 min/week |
| **Month 2** | Weekly 30-minute check-ins. Ask how onboarding is going. Connect them with practice leads. Confirm tool access is complete. | ~30 min/week |
| **Month 3** | Biweekly check-ins. Gather feedback on the onboarding program. Transition to a normal working relationship. | ~30 min/2 weeks |

Total: roughly 6–10 hours over 90 days.

## Topics to Cover

- **Focus culture and values:** outcomes over hours, empowering our clients, cameras on in a remote-first company
- **Tools and norms:** Slack norms and channels, where documentation lives, PTO calendar, Rippling, daily time tracking
- **Government context:** complex stakeholder ecosystems, why change can be slow, bringing government partners along on transformational change, and why careful delivery is how we grow
- **Further reading:** the [USDS Digital Services Playbook](https://playbook.usds.gov/), [Code for America's Benefits Playbook](https://codeforamerica.org/resources/benefits-playbook/), and the [OECD Public Sector Innovation Playbook](https://oecd-opsi.org/playbook-intro/)

## Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Be approachable and responsive | Act as their manager or assign work |
| Share your own onboarding experience | Handle performance concerns (send those to their manager) |
| Check in even when they don't ask | Share confidential information about other employees |
| Make "dumb questions" safe to ask | Make promises about projects, promotions, or compensation |
| Celebrate small wins | Skip a check-in without rescheduling |
