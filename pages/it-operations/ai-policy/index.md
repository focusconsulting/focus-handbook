# Focus AI Policy

_Company-wide policy for using, deploying, and managing AI tools and agents. Reviewed annually by the CTO._

This policy covers all employees and contractors using, deploying, or managing AI tools and systems as part of their work at Focus.

- Only use Focus-approved generative AI tools or agents with accounts managed by the CTO — or client-approved tools on a project.
- Do not input, upload, copy, or expose sensitive or confidential information to AI tools outside an approved system or client-approved environment.
- AI features built into approved systems may be used with information already stored there, when consistent with client guidance and other policies.
- Verify all AI-generated outputs for accuracy, appropriateness, bias, security risks, intellectual property, and alignment with Focus's values and client requirements.
- Using AI tooling that is not [approved](#approved-tooling) is a fireable offense.

### Why

- AI use presents opportunities for us to be more efficient in areas like data analysis, content generation, and problem solving.
- AI introduces ethical and legal risk to our business because of the increased potential for data exposure, data breaches, misinformation, bias, and trust erosion.
- AI is not thinking, but masquerades as if it has judgement.
- More agentic flows give more space for AI to misinterpret intent.

### AI Agents

AI agents can take action against real systems, and when our intent is unclear they can easily drift into performing unexpected actions with negative consequences. This nuance means that these guidelines must be strictly adhered to, and projects cannot deviate from them.

- Limit agents to the minimum set of permissions and data.
- Do not allow agents to access sensitive data or production systems.
- Require human approval before an agent:
  - Sends, shares, or publishes any messages or content.
  - Adds, changes, or modifies content or code outside of a local or draft context.
- Any actions by an AI agent are the responsibility of a single individual.
- All AI-generated content must be explainable by the person who generated it.

### Software Development

- Use Claude Code or a client-approved tool for any source code generation, modification, or agentic coding.
- All code generated by an AI must be reviewed line by line by a human.
- Technical queries (i.e., brainstorming or debugging) can leverage any approved tooling.

### Usage on Client Projects

- Comply with any client-specific policies and guidance regarding AI usage, disclosure, and approved tools & agents.
  - These should be described in the project onboarding & wrapper documents.
- For specific project policies, refer to that project's AI Policy Addendum.
  - All authored addendums or changes must be explicitly approved by the CTO and the responsible team member.
- Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) should not be entered into AI tools unless given explicit permission by the client, as documented in a project's AI Policy Addendum.

### Professional Development

Team members may freely explore AI tools and topics, provided only publicly available data is used.

### New Tooling Approval Process

- Approval by the CTO (and client, for client-specific tools) is required for other AI tools or significant new AI applications of existing tools.
- Conduct a proportionate risk assessment guided by the [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) for new AI tools, agents, or applications.
- When assessing a tool, it can be helpful to determine which of two categories the tool's publisher belongs to:
  - **Reputable institutions** that would suffer more reputational harm than Focus if the software misbehaved or was manipulated. When AWS or GitHub go down, our clients understand that Focus made a sensible decision relying on those services and generally do not blame us for their failures. Similarly, if a piece of Apple software has a bug that leaks location data, we would expect that to reflect on Apple rather than Focus. When Focus's interests align with a reputable publisher, we can be more confident in their tooling.
  - **Less well-known publishers** (smaller companies, individual engineers) are more susceptible to misbehavior and manipulation because they are smaller and have less at stake. An individual engineer may publish a neat agentic coding harness, and if they're offered $500,000 to ship telemetry to a data broker they may choose to do that. We need to thoroughly vet tools from both categories of publisher, but we also likely want to pin tools to certain vetted versions for this second category.
- Approval confirms that the tool will not use any input data or context for model training.

## Code of Conduct

- Effort to produce must exceed the effort to review.
- Do not send or distribute any AI-generated content that has not been reviewed.
- Be honest about how AI was used in any content generation.
- Understand how a tool works before weaving it into any client work, to prevent adversarial attacks or data poisoning.

### Analysis of Use

Like any new tool, we aim to make sure that its implementation is leading to the desired outcomes; individuals and teams implementing these tools are responsible for measuring the efficacy of their use.

## Definitions

- **Providers**: Companies that build and serve AI models (e.g., Anthropic, Google, OpenAI).
- **Models**: The underlying AI systems a provider trains and runs (e.g., Claude Opus, Gemini); what actually generates output.
- **Clients**: The applications or interfaces used to access a model (e.g., Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Codex). *(Distinct from a customer "client.")*
- **MCP Servers**: Connectors that give an AI client access to external tools, data, or systems (e.g., a repo, calendar, or browser) via the Model Context Protocol.
- **Offline/Local**: Models run on Focus-controlled hardware rather than a provider's cloud, so no data leaves the machine (e.g., via Ollama, LM Studio).
- **AI Agent**: An AI that takes actions in a loop to pursue a goal, not just generating content — meaning it can affect real systems with limited human input.

## Roles

- **CTO**: Approves tools, exceptions, and new-tool risk assessments; owns this policy and its review.
- **Team members**: Follow this policy, request access to approved tools, and remain accountable for all AI-assisted work they produce.
- **Managers**: Ensure their team's awareness of, compliance with, and training on this policy; serve as the first point of contact for questions.
